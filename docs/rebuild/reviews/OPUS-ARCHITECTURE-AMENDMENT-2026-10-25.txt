Required changes
The question goes into generation. The bounded input-ASR text for the turn’s synthetic clip becomes Q. It is labeled as an ASR transcript in the caption and is sent to Responses and to every check. If the transcript is empty, garbled or in an unexpected language, the turn becomes a clarify turn, not a guess.
Scoped fixed passage lookup. Passages are built from the pinned sample: the question sentence, the printed fiscal note, bullets and conditions, the application’s effect texts (labeled as such), scope limits and the dated status record. Each passage carries its version, span, retrieved_utc and a review_before date. Contest binding stays deterministic application state.
Written release of A. A is checked against Q and its cited passages:
Deterministic: citations come from this request’s bundle; numbers and units per claim; polarity only from effect passages; currentness only from dated, unexpired passages; jurisdiction; no advice or links.
Decisions predicates: entailed, no additions, responsive to Q (abstaining is allowed), neutral, attributed, language.
A, once admitted, may be shown as text. It is also the plan and coverage constraint for speech.
Spoken release of S, independently. S is the independent ASR of the buffered audio for the candidate segment. It gets the same deterministic checks and predicates run directly against Q and the pinned passages, with no A in the review input. A then constrains S only by narrowing it:
S may not cite or imply passages outside A’s cited set;
S must carry every qualifier A’s claims mark as required, such as conditions, “estimate”, polarity and attribution.
Cross-check S against Live’s own output transcript on critical tokens: numbers, negations, Yes/No, and names of measures or jurisdictions. Any disagreement holds the segment. Agreement between two model outputs lowers the risk; it is not proof.
Segment protocol, since Live has no end-of-response event.
Release unit: one candidate segment, bounded by quiet and a stable transcript, released only if the segment passes item 4 on its own. That includes standalone coverage of what Q needs, so a cut-off prefix missing its qualifier fails.
Continuation: voiced output after a released segment within the same turn is buffered as a new candidate and checked alone, or discarded. Segments are never concatenated after release.
Outside the window: output not tied to the current commentary, or arriving after Stop, new input or the deadline, is discarded.
Promise: nothing is played until that audio’s segment has passed. Nothing in the protocol proves the answer was complete, and already-heard audio cannot be retracted. Completeness is carried by A on screen.
Context holds only released content. Replace line 266: the next turn’s context is the released segment texts S plus their passage IDs. A held turn contributes nothing.
Remove the shape rules. Remove the brief character limits, the Proposition A word requirements, the bare “yes”/”sí” ban, reading_prefix_possible and the equality loops. Keep the numeric, polarity-by-citation, currentness, advice and link checks.
Accounting. Add the closed stages input_transcription and speech_review with explicit pricing entries, and recompute package attempt limits for the per-turn calls.

Whole-answer safety versus latency

Guaranteed by design: unchecked audio never plays, and every played segment is checked against Q and the pinned passages.

Not guaranteed:

that the spoken answer is complete;
that speech starts within any particular time;
any latency figure, until measured. Time to first released audio is at least the segment’s speech duration plus the boundary wait, ASR and review.

The written answer A carries the timely, complete version.
